How to download an HLS (.m3u8) video on Windows

Many sites stream video as an HLS playlist, a small .m3u8 file that points to hundreds of short pieces. You can’t just save it, because the file is only a list. Here’s how to turn one into a normal MP4 on Windows, and what won’t work.

What an .m3u8 file really is

HLS is a way of streaming video. Instead of one big file, the video is cut into hundreds of short pieces, and a small text file, the .m3u8 playlist, lists them in order. Your browser reads the playlist and fetches the pieces one by one.

That’s why right-click, Save As doesn’t work: you only get the little playlist, not the video. To end up with a normal MP4 you have to download every piece and join them in order. A related format, DASH, uses a .mpd playlist and works the same way.

The easy way: Vantload and its Chrome extension

  1. Install Vantload and add the Chrome extension.
  2. Open the page and start playing the video, so the page asks for its playlist.
  3. Click the Vantload icon in Chrome’s toolbar. Under Found on this page you’ll see the playlists it spotted (tagged HLS or DASH), plus any video files, audio and subtitles. The Download button over the video offers the same list.
  4. Press Download next to the playlist. Vantload’s confirmation window opens; choose where to save it and start.

Behind the scenes, Vantload hands the playlist to yt-dlp and FFmpeg, both included in the installer. They fetch several pieces at a time and join them into an MP4. Vantload also passes along the page that loaded the playlist, so servers that only answer to that page don’t reply with a 403 error. That needs Vantload 1.0.5 or later.

Already have the .m3u8 address? Use the extension, not Add URL. Pasting a playlist address into Add URL saves only the small playlist file.

If you see several playlists

One video can show up more than once, for example with one playlist per quality. Vantload downloads whichever one you choose. If the result is lower quality than you wanted, try another entry.

What won’t work

  • DRM-protected streams (the kind used by paid streaming services). They’re encrypted so that only an approved player can decrypt them. Vantload doesn’t try to get around that, and neither does any legitimate downloader.
  • Streams that only work while you’re signed in. Vantload doesn’t read your browser’s cookies, so these may fail with a 403 error.
  • Links that expire within minutes. Start the download right after you press play.
  • Videos in an embedded player. The page button may not appear on those, but the toolbar icon’s list still shows what the page loaded.

Only download what you’re allowed to

This is for video you have the right to save: your own recordings, material you’ve licensed, public-domain or openly licensed content, and anything else the owner permits. Respect each site’s terms and your local copyright law.
